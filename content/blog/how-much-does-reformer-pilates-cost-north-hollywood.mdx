## The short answer

At Bodies and Pilates, a single class is $36, and intro offers, packs and memberships bring the per-class cost down. Almost nobody pays the single-class rate more than once. Here is the full picture, with [our real prices](/pricing), so you can decide what fits your schedule and budget.

## Intro offers: the cheapest way to start

If you have never taken a class with us, start here. Your first class is $25, a full 50-minute small-group session, not a shortened teaser. If you want a real trial period, the one-week unlimited intro is $105 and lets you take as many classes as you want for seven days. Both are first-timers only.

Intro offers exist for one reason: Pilates is hard to judge from a single visit. Your body needs three or four classes before the movements start to click. The week unlimited is the better value if your schedule allows it, because frequency in that first week is what turns curiosity into a habit.

## Class packs vs memberships

Once the intro period ends, you have two paths.

Class packs are the flexible option. A 5-pack is $160 ($32 per class) and a 10-pack is $300 ($30 per class). Packs suit people with unpredictable schedules, shift workers, or anyone splitting time between workouts.

Memberships are the committed option and the better per-class value. Our monthly plans are $130 for 5 classes, $170 for 8 classes, $220 for 12 classes, and $280 for unlimited. The 8-class plan is our most popular: at $170 a month it works out to about $21 per class if you go twice a week, which is the cadence where most clients see real change. Memberships can be [paused anytime](/faq#pause-cancel), and you can cancel with 7 days' notice. No fees, no fine print.

The honest math: if you plan to come twice a week or more, a membership beats packs every time. If you come once a week or less, packs are usually the smarter buy.

## Private sessions and duets

Group classes are the core of what we do, but some clients start or supplement with private training. A [1:1 private session](/classes/private) is $100 for 55 minutes. Duets, where two people share a session, are $70 per person. Privates make sense if you are brand new and want a foundation, if you are working around an injury, or if you simply want undivided coaching. Many clients do a handful of privates and then move into group classes with much better form.

## What actually drives the price

Why does reformer Pilates cost more than a big-box gym class? Three things.

First, equipment. A studio-quality reformer is a serious piece of machinery; ours are professional Merrithew reformers, and a room of them represents a real capital investment. That cost is spread across every class.

Second, class size. Our classes are capped at 7 people, which means fewer clients splitting the instructor's time. You are paying for coaching, not just floor space. A 30-person fitness class and a small-group reformer session are different products at different prices.

Third, instructor attention. In a good reformer class, the instructor corrects your form throughout. That level of coaching is closer to semi-private training than to a group fitness class, and it is priced accordingly.

When you compare studios, compare these three things, not just the sticker price. A cheaper class in a packed room with no corrections is not the same product.

## How to pick the right option

New to Pilates entirely: start with the $25 first class, then the $105 week unlimited if you liked it. Coming twice a week: the $170 8-class membership. Coming three or more times a week: unlimited at $280. Unpredictable schedule: the 10-pack. Want personal attention: start with a private or two, then join group classes.

And if you are still unsure, that is what the $25 first class is for. Come take one, talk to your instructor after, and pick the plan that matches the schedule you will actually keep.
