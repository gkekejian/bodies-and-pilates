This is one of the most common questions we get, and it deserves a careful answer. Many people do continue Pilates during pregnancy, and it can be a good way to stay strong and mobile. But pregnancy is not the time for general internet advice. Start with your doctor, then talk to us.

## The short answer

Yes, many pregnant clients do Pilates, with two conditions: clearance from their doctor or midwife, and modifications from an instructor who knows they are pregnant. Those two things are non-negotiable. Everything below assumes both are in place.

## Talk to your doctor first, then tell us

Before booking, get explicit clearance from your healthcare provider, and ask whether they have any specific restrictions for you. Then tell your instructor you are pregnant before your first class, ideally when you book. This is not information to mention casually mid-class. Knowing in advance lets your instructor plan modifications for the exercises that need them and keep an eye on your form throughout.

If anything feels wrong during class, stop. That rule applies to everyone, and doubly to you right now.

## How classes get modified

A qualified instructor will adjust your workout as your pregnancy progresses. Common modifications include avoiding lying flat on your back for extended periods in later stages, skipping deep abdominal flexion work, reducing intensity, and adding support where balance gets tricky. The reformer is useful here because the springs and straps can assist movements that would be difficult unsupported.

This is also why class size matters. In a small room, your instructor can track your modifications without disrupting the class. In a packed room, you are on your own to remember every adjustment, which is not where you want your attention.

## Why privates or duets can be a better start

If you are new to Pilates and pregnant, or if your pregnancy is high-risk or complicated in any way, a private session is usually the better starting point. One-on-one, your instructor can assess how you move, build a session around your doctor's guidance, and teach you the modifications you will use in group classes later. [Our private sessions](/classes/private) are $100 for 55 minutes, and [duets are $70 per person](/pricing) if you want to bring a partner or friend.

Even experienced practitioners often do a private or two when they become pregnant, just to recalibrate. It is a sensible investment, not an upsell.

## What to look for in a studio

If you are comparing studios in North Hollywood while pregnant, ask whether the instructors have experience teaching prenatal clients and what the maximum class size is. You want an instructor who has made these modifications before, not one figuring it out in real time, and a room small enough that they can watch you specifically. A studio that answers these questions confidently is a studio you can trust with this.

## What to wear and bring

The usual guidance applies: form-fitting clothes, grip socks (required, sold at our front desk), water, and arriving ten minutes early. Eat a light snack beforehand if your provider is fine with it; low blood sugar and exercise are a bad combination at the best of times.

## The bottom line

Pilates and pregnancy can go well together, with medical clearance and an instructor in the loop.
