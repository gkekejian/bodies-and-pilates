It is the question we hear most at Bodies and Pilates, usually right after someone's first class: how often do I need to do this for it to actually work? The honest answer is that it depends on what you mean by results, but the pattern is remarkably consistent across the clients we see in our North Hollywood studio. Here is what we tell people.

## Why consistency beats intensity

Pilates is a skill practice as much as a workout. The first thing that changes is not your muscles, it is your nervous system learning to recruit them. That kind of adaptation responds to frequency, not heroics. One punishing class a month will leave you sore and not much else. Two moderate classes a week, repeated for two months, will change how you move.

This is worth internalizing because the fitness world sells intensity. Pilates rewards the opposite: showing up, paying attention, and letting small improvements compound.

## Once a week: maintenance mode

One class a week is a fine place to start, especially if you are active in other ways. You will keep the movement patterns fresh. What you will not get is momentum. Progress at once a week is real but slow, and most people find it harder to stay motivated when the changes are subtle.

If once a week is what your schedule allows, do it without guilt. It counts. Just know what it buys you.

## Twice a week: the sweet spot

This is where most of our clients land, and it is not a coincidence that our most popular membership is [eight classes a month](/pricing).

Twice weekly is also sustainable. It leaves room for rest, for the rest of your life, and for the occasional missed week without losing everything you built. If you want to get stronger and move better, aim for two classes a week. That is our most popular plan: 8 classes a month for $170.

## Three or more times a week: acceleration

Some clients come three, four, even five times a week. Some have a specific goal. Others simply love it. For them, unlimited is $280 a month. Pilates is low-impact, so higher frequency is forgiving in a way that daily heavy lifting is not.

The caveat: more is not automatically better. If you are dragging through class three, you would get more from two good sessions than three tired ones. Listen to your body, and talk to your instructor about programming if you want to ramp up.

## How long until you see results?

Every body's timeline is different. What we can promise is the setup: 50-minute classes, no more than 7 people, and an experienced instructor coaching your form. Nobody can promise you a specific body on a specific date, and you should distrust anyone who does.

## How to build the habit

[Pick two recurring times](/schedule) and protect them like appointments, because they are. Take the same instructor's classes when you can; a teacher who knows your body coaches you better every week. Give it a couple of months before you judge it. If life gets busy, [pause your membership](/faq#pause-cancel) instead of quitting. Pausing is free, and you can do it anytime.
