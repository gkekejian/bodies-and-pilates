Choosing a Pilates studio is a commitment of money, time, and trust in someone's hands on your body. Most people pick based on proximity and a nice Instagram grid, then wonder why they quit after a month. Here are seven things to check before you join any studio, with our own answer to each. Number one is class size, and ours is capped at 7.

## 1. Class size, stated plainly

This is the number that matters most and the one studios are vaguest about. On a reformer, there is a limit to how many people one instructor can genuinely coach. Beyond it, you are following along, not being taught. Ask directly: what is the maximum class size? If the answer is evasive, that is your answer. Ours is 7. [Here is why class size matters](/blog/why-small-pilates-classes-get-better-results).

## 2. The same instructors, week after week

A teacher who sees you every Tuesday learns your body: your tight hip flexor, the shoulder that creeps up, the exercise that finally clicked last month. A rotating cast of strangers starts from zero every class. Ask whether instructors have regular schedules or rotate randomly.

## 3. Published pricing

A studio confident in its value publishes its prices. When pricing is hidden behind a "contact us" wall, it usually means the sales process does the convincing that the product cannot. You should be able to see the drop-in rate, the packs, and the memberships before you ever walk in, and the per-class math should make sense. At Bodies and Pilates, the full menu is on the [pricing page](/pricing), down to the per-class cost of each membership.

## 4. A real, visible schedule

You should be able to see when classes run before you create an account anywhere. A studio with six classes a week might be perfect for your schedule or completely incompatible, and you deserve to know which before the sales conversation. Look for a [live timetable](/schedule), not a PDF from last season.

## 5. A beginner pathway

If every class on the schedule assumes experience, beginners are being set up to feel lost and quit. A serious studio has an explicit on-ramp: a Beginner class, an intro offer, or a foundations series. The presence of a beginner track tells you the studio thinks about progression, which tells you it thinks about its clients as long-term relationships rather than transactions.

## 6. Cancellation and membership terms in writing

Read the fine print before you need it. What is the late-cancel window? What happens on a no-show? Can you pause a membership, and what notice does cancellation require? Reasonable terms [look like ours](/faq#cancellation): cancel 12 or more hours ahead for no charge, credit forfeited inside 12 hours, $15 no-show fee, pause anytime, cancel with 7 days' notice and no fees. If a studio cannot state its terms plainly on its website, expect friction later.

## 7. Reviews that name names

Star ratings are a starting point. The useful reviews name specific instructors and describe specific experiences: who corrected their form, who remembered their injury, whose class they plan their week around. A pattern of named-instructor praise is the strongest signal a studio has a real coaching culture rather than a nice interior.

## Choosing a Pilates studio in Los Angeles

Los Angeles has no shortage of reformer classes, and they run on two very different models. Large franchise rooms commonly run 12 to 20 reformers in a single class. Small independent studios run far fewer. Both can be good workouts, but they are not the same workout. In a big room the instructor teaches from the front and you follow the cues. In a small one the instructor walks the room, checks your springs, and puts a hand on the shoulder that is creeping up. Our group classes are capped at 7 people, so across 50 minutes everyone gets corrected, not just the person nearest the front.

Class size also changes what you can safely try. When someone is watching your setup and alignment, you can take a heavier spring or a harder variation sooner, because a mistake gets caught in the moment instead of repeated for a month.

Look at the equipment while you are there. Professional reformers are built for all-day studio use and adjust for different bodies: footbar height, strap length, spring load. We teach on Merrithew V2 Max reformers and stability chairs, and you can see how each format uses them in [the classes we teach](/classes).

Be honest about the drive. A great studio across town tends to lose to traffic by the third week, so pick one you can reach on a normal weekday. Bodies and Pilates is in North Hollywood, on Vineland Ave, and we serve North Hollywood, Toluca Lake, Studio City, Valley Village, Burbank, Sherman Oaks, Universal City, Valley Glen, and Van Nuys. If you are comparing Los Angeles Pilates studios from one of those neighborhoods, we are likely a short trip.

Finally, try a class before you commit to a membership. Most studios offer an intro option; ours is a $25 first class, and every price is on [our pricing page](/pricing). Then run the visit test below.

## The visit test

After the research, do one thing: take the intro class and notice how you feel walking out. Did anyone learn your name? Did the instructor correct you at least once? Could you picture yourself coming back twice a week? A studio can pass all seven checks on paper and still not be your studio. The right one feels like somewhere you belong by the end of hour one.
