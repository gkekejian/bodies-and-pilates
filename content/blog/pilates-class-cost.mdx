## Why Pilates costs more than a big-box gym

Pilates pricing reflects three real costs that a $30/month gym doesn't carry:

1. **Equipment.** A professional reformer costs several thousand dollars, and a studio needs one per student, maintained and replaced over time.
2. **Small class sizes.** A boutique studio caps classes so the instructor can watch and correct every body in the room. Fewer spots per class means each spot costs more — and is worth more.
3. **Certified instruction.** Comprehensive Pilates certification takes hundreds of training hours. You're paying for someone who can adapt every exercise to your back, your knees, and your goals.

## Drop-in vs. pack vs. membership: which saves you money?

The math depends entirely on frequency:

- **Trying Pilates or visiting town?** Pay the drop-in rate ($36 with us), or better, use an intro offer — our first class is $25.
- **Once a week?** A 5- or 10-class pack drops the per-class cost to $30–$32 with no monthly commitment.
- **Twice a week?** The 8-class membership ($170/month) beats packs at $21.25 per class.
- **Three or more times a week?** Unlimited ($280/month) wins. At 12 classes a month you're paying about $23 per class; at 16, about $17.50.

Most people feel noticeable change practicing 2–3 times per week — which is why the 8- and 12-class memberships are our most popular options.

## What about private sessions?

Private sessions ($100 at our studio) cost roughly three times a group class, and for two groups of people they're worth every dollar: complete beginners who want a confident foundation before joining a group, and anyone working around an injury, pregnancy, or a specific goal. Many clients book 1–3 privates first, then move into group classes.

## Hidden costs to ask any studio about

- **Grip socks** — required at nearly every reformer studio (including ours) for safety and hygiene. Bring your own or buy a pair at the front desk.
- **Late-cancel and no-show fees** — ours are simple: cancel at least 12 hours ahead, or a no-show costs $35. Every studio has a version of this; read it before you book.
- **Expiration windows** — class packs almost always expire. Ours last three months, which is on the generous side for LA.

## Is Pilates worth the price?

If you'll actually go, yes. Pilates delivers strength, flexibility, and posture work in one low-impact session, scales from total beginner to advanced athlete, and — in a small class — comes with the kind of individual correction you'd otherwise pay personal-training rates for. The honest comparison isn't Pilates vs. a gym membership; it's Pilates vs. a gym membership you don't use.
