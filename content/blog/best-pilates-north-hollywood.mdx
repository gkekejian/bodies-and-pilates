## What separates a great Pilates studio from an average one

After teaching thousands of sessions, we'd tell a friend to judge any studio — including ours — on five things:

### 1. Class size

This is the single biggest quality lever. In a 6–10 person class, the instructor sees every rep and corrects your form in real time. In a 20+ person room, you're following along with a workout video that happens to be live. Ask the studio directly: *how many reformers are in the room?*

### 2. Instructor certification and attention

Comprehensively certified instructors (hundreds of hours of training, not a weekend course) know how to modify for your lower back, your wrists, your pregnancy, your marathon training. The tell: in your first class, does the instructor learn your name and ask about injuries before you start?

### 3. Beginner pathway

A good studio has a real answer for "I've never done this before" — a dedicated beginner class, an intro private, or both. If every class is "all levels" and moves at one speed, beginners sink.

### 4. Honest, published pricing

Every price on the website, intro offer included, with cancellation policies in plain English. Studios that make you "come in to discuss membership options" are selling you, not teaching you.

### 5. The room itself

Clean equipment, natural light, enough space between reformers, and a front desk that greets you. You'll spend hours here; it should feel like a place you *want* to be at 7 AM.
