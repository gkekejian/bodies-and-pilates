**If you've never touched a reformer, here's the reassuring truth:** the machine was designed to *help* you. Joseph Pilates built it so springs could support your body while you learn correct movement — which is why reformer Pilates is one of the best entry points into fitness for absolute beginners, people coming back from a break, and anyone working around stiffness or old injuries.

Here's everything you need to know before your first class.

## What is a reformer, exactly?

A reformer is a padded carriage that slides along a frame, connected to adjustable springs. You push or pull the carriage using a footbar and long straps, and the springs create resistance — or assistance — depending on the exercise. Heavier springs aren't always harder; sometimes a lighter spring forces your core to do more stabilizing. Your instructor sets this for you, every exercise, so you never have to guess.

## What happens in your first class

At Bodies and Pilates, a beginner class runs about 50 minutes and follows a predictable arc:

1. **Setup and intros.** Arrive 10 minutes early — you'll sign a waiver, meet your instructor, and tell them about any injuries or limitations. This conversation matters; it's how they'll tailor your class.
2. **Footwork warm-up.** Nearly every reformer class starts lying on your back, pressing the carriage out with your feet on the footbar. It's gentle, rhythmic, and teaches you how the springs feel.
3. **The fundamentals.** Slow, coached movements for core, legs, arms, and back. Beginner classes move deliberately — the goal is precision, not exhaustion.
4. **Stretch and finish.** Reformer stretching is one of its quiet joys; the straps and carriage let you stretch deeper, supported.

Expect to feel worked but not wrecked — and expect muscles you didn't know you had to introduce themselves the next morning.

## What to wear and bring

- **Form-fitting workout clothes.** Your instructor needs to see your alignment to correct it; baggy clothing hides it and can catch in the equipment.
- **Grip socks — required.** Every reputable reformer studio requires them for safety and hygiene. We sell them at the front desk if you forget.
- **Water.** That's it. Mats and all equipment are provided.

## Five beginner tips from our instructors

1. **Tell us everything.** Bad knee, tight shoulders, pregnancy, nerves — the more your instructor knows, the better your class gets.
2. **Slower is stronger.** Racing through reps recruits momentum; moving slowly recruits muscle. If a movement feels easy fast, try it slow.
3. **Breathe on purpose.** Exhale on the effort. Your instructor will cue it constantly; it's half the method.
4. **Don't compare carriages.** The person next to you might be on their 200th class. Springs make every body's class different by design.
5. **Come back within a week.** The vocabulary clicks fast when classes are close together. Two classes in your first week beats one class in each of two weeks.

## Common beginner worries, answered

**"I'm not flexible enough for Pilates."** Flexibility is an *outcome* of Pilates, not a prerequisite. Springs assist your range while you build it.

**"I'm not fit enough."** The reformer scales down as far as you need. Our beginner class exists precisely for fundamentals at a humane pace.

**"Will I be lost?"** In a small class, no one stays lost — your instructor is watching and will reset you in seconds. This is the biggest advantage of boutique class sizes over 20-machine rooms.

**"I'm pregnant — can I join?"** With doctor's clearance, yes. Tell your instructor, and consider starting with 1–3 private sessions; we'll tailor everything from there.
