Reformer Pilates and yoga get grouped together constantly. Both are mindful, both are low-impact, both attract people who want strength without pounding their joints. Studios like ours in North Hollywood get the question weekly: should I do Pilates or yoga?

The honest answer is that they train different qualities, they feel different in the room, and a lot of people happily do both. Here is how to think about it.

## What each one trains

Yoga trains mobility, balance, breath control, and bodyweight strength through poses held and linked in sequences. The resistance comes from your own body and gravity, and flexibility is a central goal, not a side effect.

Reformer Pilates trains controlled strength, core stability, posture, and coordination using spring resistance. The reformer's springs add load to movements, so you are doing genuine resistance training, just without barbells. Flexibility improves too, but it arrives through loaded stretching and full-range movement rather than long static holds.

## How a class feels

A yoga class typically flows. You move through sequences at the teacher's pace, hold poses, breathe, and settle into a rhythm. There is often a meditative quality, especially in slower styles.

A [reformer Pilates class at Bodies and Pilates](/classes) is more segmented. Your instructor sets up each exercise, coaches your form rep by rep, and adjusts springs between blocks. It is quieter and more technical. You will hear a lot of cueing about ribs, pelvis, shoulders, and breath, because precision is the point. Our classes run 50 minutes in small groups so the instructor can actually watch you move.

## Who tends to prefer which

People who love the flow state, the breathwork, and the flexibility focus usually lean yoga. People who want measurable strength progression, postural correction, and hands-on form coaching usually lean reformer Pilates.

Desk workers with tight hips and rounded shoulders often do well starting with reformer Pilates, because the coached, resistance-based work rebuilds the strength patterns that sitting erodes. People seeking stress relief and mobility sometimes start with yoga. These are tendencies, not rules.

## Why many people do both

They complement each other almost perfectly. Pilates builds the core strength and stability that make yoga poses safer and stronger. Yoga builds the mobility and breath awareness that make Pilates movements smoother. Two reformer sessions plus one or two yoga sessions a week is one of the most balanced routines a person can build, and it is gentle enough to sustain for years.

If you are choosing one to start with, pick the one whose class description excites you more. Consistency beats optimization every time. The best workout is the one you will still be doing in six months.

## A note on which is harder

People ask this constantly, and the answer is unsatisfying: it depends on the class and the teacher. A vigorous vinyasa class will humble a Pilates regular, and an advanced reformer class will humble a yogi. Difficulty lives in the programming, not the method. If you want the hardest version of either, tell the instructor you are experienced and want to be challenged. Both disciplines have deep water.
