At Bodies and Pilates, a [private session](/classes/private) is $100 for 55 minutes, a duet is $70 per person, and a group class is $36 drop-in or less on a membership. Those numbers raise an obvious question: is the private worth nearly three times the group class? Sometimes. Here is an honest breakdown.

## What you actually get in a private

A private session is fifty-five minutes of one person's undivided expertise applied entirely to your body. Every exercise is chosen for you, every cue is about your alignment, and the pace adjusts rep by rep. If you have an injury history, a specific goal like returning to sport, or significant anxiety about group settings, that level of attention is genuinely valuable.

Privates are also a focused way to learn, because every minute is instructional.

## What you actually get in a small group

Here is the part the industry does not always say clearly: in a genuinely small group, with an instructor who coaches rather than performs, you get most of the benefit at a fraction of the price. Our group classes are small enough that the instructor corrects form, learns your body over weeks, and progresses you individually. You also get something privates cannot offer: the energy of moving alongside other people, which keeps a remarkable number of clients consistent.

The group experience fails when classes are too large for the instructor to see everyone. That is a class-size problem, not a format problem, which is why it is worth asking any studio how many reformers are in the room before you judge group Pilates by a bad crowded experience elsewhere.

## Who should book privates

Book privates if you are working around an injury or medical condition (with your provider's clearance) or if you tried group classes and felt lost. A common smart pattern: two to four privates to build a foundation, then transition into group classes with a huge head start.

## Who thrives in groups

Almost everyone else. If you are generally healthy, comfortable learning at a normal pace, and motivated by routine and community, small-group classes are the best value in Pilates. The per-class cost on our [$170 eight-class membership](/pricing) works out to about $21, which is exceptional for coached, equipment-based training.

## The duet middle path

Duets, at $70 per person, split the difference nicely. You get a semi-private level of attention, you bring a partner or friend which makes it more fun and more accountable, and the price sits between group and private. They are popular with couples, friends starting together, and pairs with similar goals or limitations.

## The honest math

If budget is the constraint, eight group classes a month will do more for your body than two privates. Frequency beats intensity of attention. If you want undivided attention, a private is $100 for 55 minutes. Most of our long-term clients end up in groups, with an occasional private when they want a tune-up or are working through something specific. That hybrid is probably the smartest Pilates budget there is.

And if you are still unsure, start with the $25 first class. One group session will tell you more about what you need than any article can. Your instructor can see in a single class whether you would benefit from a few privates or whether you are ready to thrive in the group setting.
