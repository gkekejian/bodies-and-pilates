Starting Pilates as a beginner in North Hollywood means choosing between a dozen studios, each promising something slightly different. This is a roadmap for doing it well: what to look for, where to begin, and what your first month should actually look like.

## Start with the right class, not just any class

The single biggest beginner mistake is walking into an advanced class because the time was convenient. Reformer Pilates has a learning curve, and the first few sessions are about learning the machine, the vocabulary, and how your body responds to spring resistance.

That is exactly why Bodies and Pilates runs a dedicated [Beginner class](/classes/beginner). It moves at a teaching pace, the instructor explains the setup for every exercise, and nobody expects you to know what a footbar is. Start there, even if you are fit from other sports. Fitness does not transfer as directly as people assume; the reformer humbles runners and lifters alike.

## What to look for in a studio

Visit before you commit. You are looking for small class sizes, because a beginner needs eyes on their form, and that is impossible in a room of thirty. Ask whether the same instructors teach week after week; a teacher who sees you regularly will progress you faster than a rotating cast. Check that pricing is published and plain, and read reviews that name specific instructors. Those details tell you more than any marketing copy.

Our studio on Vineland Ave caps classes at 7 people for exactly this reason. When you are new, the instructor should know your name and your limitations by the end of your first session.

## What your first month should look like

Week one: the [One-Week Unlimited ($105)](/pricing). Take several Beginner classes in your first seven days. Your only job is to learn the equipment and find your footing. You may be sore in places you did not know existed, which is normal.

After that: a membership. Most people settle on two classes a week, which is our most popular plan, 8 classes a month for $170. Keep taking Beginner until your instructor suggests Full Body. You can pause anytime and cancel with 7 days' notice, no fees.

## What to bring and what to wear

Wear form-fitting clothes you can move in, nothing baggy enough to catch on the equipment. Grip socks are required on the reformer for safety and hygiene; we sell them at the front desk if you do not have a pair. Bring water. That is the whole list.

[Parking](/faq#parking): metered street parking is available on Weddington St and Vineland Ave. Arrive ten minutes early for your first class so there is time for a tour and a proper reformer setup.

## When to level up

You are ready for harder classes when the Beginner exercises feel controlled rather than chaotic, when you can set up your own reformer without help, and when your instructor starts giving you the advanced variation unprompted. There is no test and no ceremony. Most people get there in four to eight weeks, and there is no prize for rushing it.

One more thing worth knowing: the beginner phase is the most fun part. Everything is new, progress is fast, and every class teaches you something. Experienced clients sometimes tell us they miss it. Enjoy the steep part of the curve while you are on it.
