## The non-negotiable: grip socks

Grip socks are required in our studio, no exceptions. Here is why. The reformer carriage is a smooth, moving surface, and several exercises have you pushing, lunging, or standing on it. Rubberized grips keep you stable and keep the equipment clean and hygienic for the next client. They are a safety item and a courtesy item at once.

If you do not own a pair, we sell them at the front desk. After your first visit, keep a pair in your gym bag so you never have to think about it again. Any brand with full-foot rubber grips works.

## Tops: fitted and stay-put

Wear a top that stays in place when you are upside down, sideways, or reclined. Fitted tees, tanks, and long-sleeve athletic tops all work. What does not work: loose t-shirts that fall over your face during rollovers, or anything you have to keep adjusting. You want to think about the exercise, not your shirt.

Sports bras with real support are worth it. Comfort here is performance.

## Bottoms: leggings win

Full-length or capri leggings are the standard for a reason. They let your instructor see your knee and hip alignment, they do not ride up or fall down, and they do not catch on the springs or straps. Bike shorts work for the same reasons if you prefer them.

Avoid: baggy sweatpants (they snag and they hide your form from your instructor), shorts that ride up during leg work in the straps, and anything with zippers, buttons, or rivets that can scratch the reformer's upholstery.

## What to avoid everywhere

Jewelry comes off before class. Rings scratch equipment, necklaces swing into your face, and bracelets catch on straps. Leave valuables at home, not on the reformer.

Strong perfume or scented lotion: the studio is a small room and everyone breathes hard. Go light or skip it.

## Hair, and the small stuff

Long hair goes up and stays up. A low bun or braid beats a high ponytail, which digs into your head when you are lying on the carriage. Bring a water bottle; having your own at your reformer means you never break your flow. That is genuinely everything you need.

## What we sell at the front desk

If you arrive missing anything, the front desk has you covered. Grip socks are required. Bring your own or buy a pair at the studio. Beyond that, everything you need for class is already in the room: the reformer, the props, and an instructor who will tell you exactly what to do.

One more practical note for planners: there is no need to arrive in full athleisure fashion. Nobody is evaluating outfits. The clients who look most at home are the ones in simple, functional clothes they do not think about. Aim for invisible, not impressive.

## The two-minute checklist

Before you leave home: fitted top, leggings, grip socks, hair tied back, jewelry off, water bottle. If you can check all six, you are dressed correctly for any class on [our schedule](/schedule). New? Start with [Beginner](/classes/beginner).

First-timers sometimes overthink this. The dress code exists to keep you safe and comfortable, not to judge your wardrobe. Show up in the checklist above and nobody will look at your clothes twice. They will be too busy shaking through the core series with you.
