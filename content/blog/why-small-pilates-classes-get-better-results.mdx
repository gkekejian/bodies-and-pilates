Pilates is a method built on precision. Small differences in alignment change which muscles do the work, which is why the size of your class matters more here than in almost any other workout. A spin class works with forty riders because the bike constrains your movement. A reformer does not constrain much. Your instructor does, and only if they can actually see you.

## The math of attention

In a class of 7, our maximum, your instructor's attention is split seven ways. In a class of twenty, it is split twenty ways. That is not a criticism of large classes; it is arithmetic. Form corrections are the product you are buying in Pilates, and attention is the limiting resource.

Consider what an instructor is watching for: pelvic position, rib placement, neck tension, spring load, breath timing, range of motion. That is six things per person. Multiply by the headcount and you see why small rooms produce better movers.

## Real-time corrections change everything

The highest-value moment in a Pilates class is the quiet correction. A hand on your shoulder blade during an arm series. A "tuck your pelvis" whispered as you set up. A spring changed mid-class because your instructor noticed you muscling through.

These moments do not happen on a schedule. They happen when an instructor has the bandwidth to watch you move and the proximity to reach you. With no more than 7 people in the room, your instructor can coach your form all class long.

## No hiding in the back row

Large fitness classes have a back row, physically and psychologically. It is where form goes to die. You know the feeling: the instructor is a distant figure, the mirror is crowded, and nobody will notice if your teaser becomes a sit-up.

Small classes remove the back row. That sounds intimidating, and for about ten minutes it is. Then it becomes the reason you improve. You cannot coast, which means every class counts. Clients are often surprised that they work harder in a small, quiet room than they ever did in a packed, loud one.

## What small actually feels like

At [Bodies and Pilates](/about), small means your instructor learns your name, your injuries, and your tendencies. They know you grip the straps too hard when you are tired. They know your left hip hikes on single-leg work. That knowledge accumulates across classes into coaching that is specific to your body, which is the closest thing group fitness gets to private training.

It also means the room stays calm. No shouting over music, no waiting for equipment, no jostling for space. [Fifty minutes](/classes), no more than 7 people, each on a professional Merrithew reformer.

## How to judge a studio's class size

When you are comparing studios in North Hollywood, ask two questions. First: what is the maximum headcount per class, not the average? Averages hide the packed Saturday morning. Second: how many reformers are in the room? The machine count is the honest cap. Our classes are capped at 7 people, and that cap is a feature.

Then take a trial class and count your corrections. If you leave a fifty-minute session without a single personalized cue, the class was too big for the method, whatever the headcount claimed.

This is the bet our whole studio is built on: fewer people, more coaching, better results. It costs more per class than a packed room, and it is worth it for the same reason a tutor beats a lecture hall.
